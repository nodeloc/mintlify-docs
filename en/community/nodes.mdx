Nodes are NodeLoc's sub-communities. Each node revolves around a topic (VPS, domains, AI, casual chat, etc.). After joining a node, you can quickly access it from the sidebar, and posting to a node helps your content reach like-minded people.

## Browsing and Joining Nodes

- Visit [Browse Nodes](https://www.nodeloc.com/nodes) to see all nodes, filterable by category (Internet services, devices & hardware, tech & creation, etc.).
- Click "Join" on a node card to become a member; you can also "Leave" from the node page.
- Node pages support three view modes: compact, expanded, and card.
- The "Nodes" section of the sidebar shows your recently visited nodes, and [My Nodes](https://www.nodeloc.com/my-nodes) collects the nodes you've created, joined, and manage.

## Creating a Node

Eligible users can create their own nodes:

| Requirement | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Trust level | Diamond member (TL3) or above |
| Limit | Up to 5 nodes per user |

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the creation wizard">
    Click "Create Node" in the sidebar's "Nodes" section, or visit [/new-node](https://www.nodeloc.com/new-node).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a parent category">
    Select the top-level category the node belongs to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in basic info">
    Node name (3~50 characters), URL slug (≤12 characters), description, etc. **The name and slug cannot be changed after creation** (only admins can change them), so choose carefully.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Appearance settings">
    Upload a node logo, set the theme color, and more.
  </Step>
</Steps>

After creation:

- The system automatically creates a member group and a management group, and automatically publishes a welcome post.
- As the node's creator, you can edit node info, set node rules, and add node links (up to 3).
- The creator **cannot leave** their own node; you can transfer ownership to another member under "Mod tools → Settings → Ownership" (see "Transferring ownership" below).

## Node Page Features

Inside a node, the right-hand info panel shows the node's description, member count, topic count, post count, and admin list, and provides:

- **Join / Leave**: manage your membership
- **About**: view the node's details
- **Node Rules**: node-specific rules set by the creator — read them before posting

## Managing your node

Node owners and moderators enter the mod tools from the "Mod tools" button in the right-hand panel of the node page, at `/n/node-slug/mod`. The tools are split into sections, one per job:

- **The node owner** (and site staff): edit the node's info, appearance and admission mode, appoint moderators, transfer ownership.
- **Moderators**: work the review queue, manage restricted users, assign user flair, pin replies, close discussion branches.

### Moderators and members

Membership is managed across three tabs:

- **Moderators**: add or remove moderators. Moderators can use the moderation features of the mod tools, but cannot change the node's settings.
- **Invite links**: generate invite links with an expiry of 1 / 7 / 30 / 90 days and 1 / 5 / 10 / 25 uses. Anyone with a link joins directly — new users register first — and the list shows how each link has been used.
- **Members**: see and manage everyone who has joined.

  "Add member" works by invitation: the person gets a single-use invite link and a private message, and only joins once they confirm. Nobody is placed in a node unasked.

A private node shows visitors a landing page with "Request to join" instead of hiding away; the owner or moderators approve or deny requests in the mod tools.

### Restricted users

Members who misbehave can be restricted per node, without affecting them anywhere else on the site:

| Restriction | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Mute | Cannot post or reply in this node for the term |
| Ban | Also leaves the node's member group and cannot rejoin during the term |

Terms run 1 / 3 / 7 / 28 / 365 days or permanent, with an optional reason (sent to the member by message). While restricted, their posts in the node are blocked with a clear explanation.

### User flair

Flair is a small label (text + emoji + colour) worn beside a member's name, only inside this node. A member can wear several at once.

- Moderators define flairs and assign them; flairs marked "mod-only" cannot be picked by members.
- When "let members pick their own flair" is on, members choose for themselves.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/screenshots/en/mod-flair.png" alt="Mod tools — user flair" />

### Links and tags

- **Links**: up to 3, shown as a small banner beside the node's topics — good for docs, chat groups or resource pages.
- **Tags**: node owners can keep tag groups of their own (tags that do not exist yet are created, no trust level required; they are usable across the whole site, not only in the node), set how many tags a new topic must carry at minimum (0 makes tags optional), and require new topics to carry tags from a chosen group. Restricting a tag to a single node is a site setting and stays with the admins.

### Channels

Connect the node to what you publish elsewhere: a new YouTube video, a Telegram post, a blog entry or a Bilibili upload becomes a topic in the node on its own. A node can connect up to 10 channels; only the node owner manages them.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/screenshots/en/mod-channels.png" alt="Mod tools — channels" />
</Frame>

Click "Connect a channel" and pick a type:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/screenshots/en/mod-channels-add.png" alt="Connect a channel — pick a type" />
</Frame>

| Type | How to connect | How it syncs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **YouTube** | Paste the channel page link, its `@handle` or the channel ID | Through YouTube's official push subscription (WebSub): a new video becomes a topic within minutes, with the video embedded. The subscription lasts 10 days and is renewed automatically |
| **Telegram** | Read the channel through your own bot | Every channel post syncs in real time, formatting such as bold and links carries over, pictures and clips are uploaded to the site, albums become one topic, and edits in the channel update the topic |
| **RSS** | Paste the RSS/Atom address of a blog, podcast or any site, or just the site's home page and the feed is discovered | Checked every 10 minutes; only items published after connecting are synced, each with the original link and a short excerpt |
| **Bilibili** | Paste the uploader's space link or UID | Read through the site's RSSHub, checked every 10 minutes; new uploads become topics with the video link |
| **Webhook** | The system issues a push address with a secret | Anything that can send an HTTP request (n8n, Zapier, IFTTT, your own script) can feed the node; sources without a public API go this way |

YouTube is push-first: while YouTube has not yet confirmed the subscription the state reads "Polling" and the channel feed is read every 10 minutes instead, switching back to push once confirmed. RSS and Bilibili channels also have a "Check now" button for when you would rather not wait.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Telegram: create a bot">
    Ask [@BotFather](https://t.me/BotFather) for a new bot and copy its token.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make it a channel administrator">
    Add the bot as an administrator of your channel; otherwise it never sees the posts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect and bind">
    Pick Telegram on the Channels page, paste the token and connect. The page shows a `/bind CODE` command; post it in the channel once to bind it. The message is deleted automatically and the code is good for an hour. The page switches to "Live" as soon as the bind lands.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/screenshots/en/mod-channels-telegram.png" alt="Binding a Telegram channel" />
</Frame>

Each channel has its own options (the pencil button):

- **Post as**: the node owner, or the site's channel bot account.
- **Publish manually**: when checked, synced items wait in the node's review queue until you approve them; by default they publish directly.
- **Posts per day, at most**: 20 by default; anything beyond is skipped and recorded.
- **Title prefix** and **tags to add**.

The list shows each channel's state (Live / Waiting for confirmation / Subscription expired / Disabled), when it last synced, any error, and the items received most recently. The rotate icon only refreshes the status; a YouTube channel waiting for confirmation shows a "Resubscribe" button, and an unbound Telegram channel can "Issue a new code".

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/screenshots/en/mod-channels-youtube.png" alt="A YouTube channel and its recent items" />
</Frame>

Synced topics carry a source badge (From YouTube / From Telegram / From RSS / From Bilibili / Synced) that links back to the original.

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/screenshots/en/channel-badge.png" alt="The source badge in the topic list" />
</Frame>

<Note>
  Opening the webhook address in a browser confirms it is valid and explains how to use it; to push, POST JSON to it: `{ "title": "...", "body": "...", "url": "original link", "external_id": "optional, for de-duplication" }`. The address carries the secret, so keep it private.
</Note>

<Note>
  Synced topics do not earn the node owner any posting reward points.
</Note>

Changes preview live and only take effect when saved. The key colour washes the whole node page — background, buttons and accents; it can also be switched off in settings, keeping the colour only on the node's disc.

### Settings

The settings page covers the node's appearance, access and posting rules, plus two tabs:

- **Official mark**: a verified merchant can apply for the official community mark on one of their nodes. Staff review the application, and the mark shows beside the node's name. The mark follows the merchant term — it lapses when the term ends or the node changes hands, and restores itself when the same owner renews.
- **Ownership**: hand the node to another member.

  <img src="/images/screenshots/en/mod-settings.png" alt="Mod tools — settings" />

#### Access and posting

"Access and posting" has two columns that decide separately what **people who have not joined** and **node members** may do here. Underneath they map onto Discourse's category permissions (the everyone group and the node's member group):

| People who have not joined | Node members |
| --- | --- |
| Cannot see this node (private node: non-members only get the intro and a join request) | Read only (only moderators post) |
| Read only | Read and reply (only moderators start topics) |
| Read and reply (starting a topic requires joining) | Start topics and reply |
| Start topics and reply | |

Non-members never get more than members: widening the non-member side pulls members up, narrowing the member side pulls non-members down. Moderators always have full rights.

### Transferring ownership

A transfer is a handshake — nothing changes the moment it is sent:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Send the offer">
    Under "Settings → Ownership", pick the member who should take over and send the offer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They confirm">
    They accept or decline on the node's transfer page (`/n/node-slug/transfer`). Until they accept, nothing changes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Handover">
    Once they accept, roles move immediately: you keep your membership but lose owner privileges.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An offer stays valid for 7 days and can be withdrawn at any time before it is accepted.

## Pinning a reply

In nested view, a reply can be lifted above all the others. It is what you want when the answer, the conclusion, or an important correction is buried forty replies down and everyone arriving late has to dig for it.

Who can pin:

- **A topic's author**, in their own topic
- **A node's creator and its moderators**, in any topic in their node
- Staff

Open the reply's actions and choose "Pin"; choosing it again unpins. Pinning only changes the order inside that one topic — it does not push the topic up any list, which is a different thing entirely.

<Note>
  Only replies made directly to the topic can be pinned, not replies nested under another reply. There is a limit on how many replies one topic can have pinned.
</Note>

## Closing one branch

Sometimes the topic is fine and only one thread of it has gone bad. Closing the whole topic to deal with that is far too blunt — **closing a branch** stops replies to that one reply and everything nested under it, and leaves the rest of the topic running.

Only a **node's creator and its moderators** (and staff) can do this — not the topic's author. Pinning is "read this one first", which is the author's to say; closing part of the conversation is moderation, and belongs to the people who answer for the node.

Open the actions on the reply and choose "Close this branch to replies"; choosing it again reopens it.

<Note>
  A close covers the **whole branch** — the forty replies already sitting under that one are closed too. That is deliberate: closing a single reply just moves the argument down one level.

  A topic's opening post cannot be closed this way. Closing the whole topic is its own operation, and it leaves a visible notice and a record.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Every node is an independent small community, with its vibe and rules maintained by its creator. Finding a node of like-minded people gets you more responses than posting in the big general categories.
</Tip>
